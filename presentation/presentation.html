What is this project for?

Purpose

Enter a wheel radius and a motor speed, then see how a scooter tire moves: its angular velocity, rim speed and centripetal acceleration.

Intended users

Grade 11 learners who want to explore rotational motion and check their own calculations.

Real-life use

Electric scooters, e-bikes and cars: the motor RPM and wheel radius decide the ground speed and the stress on the tire.

  • Purpose: a learning tool, not just a calculator.
  • Users: your classmates in Grade 11.
  • Real life: the number on a scooter's speedometer comes from RPM and wheel radius.
Concept 1 of 3

Angular velocity

ω = 2πN / 60
ωangular velocity, in rad/s (how fast the wheel turns)
Nmotor speed, in RPM (revolutions per minute)
2πone revolution = 2π rad
60one minute = 60 s
  • The motor speed is given in RPM, but physics formulas need rad/s.
  • One turn is 2π radians and one minute is 60 seconds, so ω = 2πN/60.
  • Example: 600 RPM → 62.832 rad/s.
Concept 2 of 3

Tangential speed

vt = r ω
vtspeed of a point on the tire edge, in m/s
rwheel radius, in m
ωangular velocity, in rad/s

Rolling without slipping: this is also the scooter's speed on the road.

  • The edge of a bigger wheel travels farther in one turn, so it moves faster.
  • Direction: along the circle, perpendicular to the radius (green arrow).
  • Assumption: the tire does not slip on the ground.
Concept 3 of 3

Centripetal acceleration

ac = rω² = vt² / r
acacceleration toward the center, in m/s²
rradius, in m
ωangular velocity, in rad/s

It always points to the center. It is not the same as tangential acceleration.

  • Even at constant speed the direction keeps changing, so there is an acceleration.
  • It points toward the center (orange arrow) and grows with the square of ω.
  • Doubling the RPM makes ac four times larger.

Also in the program, and the assumptions

Extra results

  • Period: T = 60 / N (s)
  • Angular displacement: θ = ωt (rad)
  • Arc length: s = rθ (m)

Assumptions

  • Constant angular velocity (α = 0)
  • Rigid wheel, fixed radius
  • Rolling without slipping
  • Direct drive, no air resistance
  • Angles in radians
  • Mention the extras quickly, then spend your time on the assumptions.
  • Because ω is constant, tangential acceleration is zero; the only acceleration is centripetal.

Three input conditions, three sets of results

Case 1 → 2: a bigger wheel at lower RPM gives about the same speed but a much smaller ac.
Case 3: doubling the RPM again makes ac very large (it grows with ω²).

  • These numbers are calculated live by the program's own formulas.
  • Say what changed between cases and why the results changed that way.
  • Rule of thumb: vt grows with r and N; ac grows with r and N².

One complete manual calculation: Case 1

      
Given: r = 0.12 m, N = 600 RPM

      
ω  = 2πN / 60 = 2π(600) / 60 = 20π ≈ 62.832 rad/s
vt = rω = (0.12)(62.832) ≈ 7.540 m/s
ac = rω² = (0.12)(62.832)² ≈ 473.741 m/s²
