Step 1 · Prepare before you press record

Ten minutes of preparation saves an hour of re-recording.

  1. Know the program. Open project/index.html and try every control: radius, unit, RPM, time, presets, the slider, the test panel and section 9.
  2. Open two windows: the analyzer in your browser, and script.js in VS Code or Notepad. Make the text big (browser zoom Ctrl + + to 125%; code font size 18 or more) so a viewer can read it.
  3. Do one manual calculation on paper first. Use Case 1 (r = 0.12 m, N = 600 RPM). You will show this on camera.
  4. Write short bullet points for each part (not a full script). Bullet points keep you natural and in your own words.
  5. Find a quiet room. Close other tabs and apps, turn on Do Not Disturb, and test your microphone with a 10-second recording.

Step 3 · What to show and say in each part

The talking points are prompts, not a script. Say them in your own words.

Part 1 · Introduction 0:00 – 0:30

Show on screen

The top of the analyzer (title and wheel), or a title slide with your name.

Talking points

  • Your name, grade, section, and the project title: E-Scooter Smart Wheel Analyzer.
  • Purpose: it lets Grade 11 learners enter a wheel radius and motor speed and see the rotational motion of a scooter tire.
  • Real-life use: scooter speedometer and tire stress.

Part 2 · Physics concepts 0:30 – 1:45

Show on screen

The Results tiles and the Model assumptions section (section 8 in the analyzer).

Talking points (say each equation and define every symbol)

  • Angular velocity ω = 2πN / 60, in rad/s. N is RPM; 1 turn = 2π rad, 1 min = 60 s.
  • Tangential speed vt = rω, in m/s. It is also the scooter's ground speed.
  • Centripetal acceleration ac = rω², in m/s², pointing to the center.
  • Extras you can mention: period T = 60/N, angular displacement θ = ωt, arc length s = rθ.
  • Assumptions: constant ω, rigid wheel, rolling without slipping, direct drive.

Part 3 · Program demonstration 1:45 – 4:00

Show on screen

The analyzer running. Use the Load buttons in section 9, or type the values.

Do this in order

  1. Point out the controls: radius with unit, RPM, time, slider, pause.
  2. Condition 1: r = 0.12 m, N = 600 RPM. Read ω, vt, ac aloud with units.
  3. Condition 2: r = 0.25 m, N = 300 RPM. Say what changed and why.
  4. Condition 3: r = 0.10 m, N = 1200 RPM. Point at the arrows and the θ–t graph.
  5. Optional: type 12 with unit cm to show unit conversion, then type 0 or a letter to show the invalid-input message.

Say the trend: doubling the RPM doubles ω and vt but makes ac four times larger.

Part 4 · Physics and coding 4:00 – 5:30

Show on screen

script.js in your editor, then section 9 of the analyzer (Validation evidence).

Talking points

  • Search the code (Ctrl + F) for angularVelocity, tangentialSpeed and centripetalAcceleration (Section 1): "this is where ω = 2πN/60 is written in code".
  • readField() (Section 2): reads each input, converts the radius to meters, rejects bad values.
  • updateAll() (Section 3): reads the inputs, calls analyze(), updates every result.
  • drawWheel() (Section 4): draws the wheel and arrows, and animate() turns it at ω × slow factor.
  • One complete manual calculation (Case 1), written on paper or shown from section 9:
    ω = 2π(600)/60 = 62.832 rad/s  ·  vt = 0.12 × 62.832 = 7.540 m/s  ·  ac = 0.12 × 62.832² = 473.741 m/s². Then show the program gives the same.
  • Show the three-case validation table (all ✔) and say what "agree at 3 decimals" means.

Part 5 · Conclusion and reflection 5:30 – 6:00

Show on screen

The Credits section (section 10) or a closing slide.

Talking points

  • Summary: three concepts, two main inputs, live animation, validated results.
  • A challenge and how you fixed it (be honest and specific): for example, a wheel spinning at 600 RPM is too fast to see, so you added a slow-motion slider and declared it.
  • A real-world use: checking tire and rim stress or computing scooter speed from motor RPM.
  • Close with credits: who did what, the equation source, and the AI assistance.

Xbox Game Bar (Win + G) records only one app at a time, which is awkward when you switch between the browser and the code editor. OBS or Clipchamp records the whole screen.

Tips for a clear video

  • Record each part separately and join them. Re-recording one part is easier than the whole video.
  • Pause on every equation, input value and unit for 2–3 seconds so it can be read.
  • Move the mouse slowly and point at what you are talking about.
  • Speak slowly. Captions are encouraged for accessibility.
  • Show your face or voice for every explanation. You are the only member, so the whole video is your explanation.
